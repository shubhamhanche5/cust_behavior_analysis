# Customer Behavior Analysis

An end-to-end data analytics project using Python, PostgreSQL, and Power BI to explore purchasing patterns, product performance, and customer behavior.
Overview
This project transforms raw shopping data into insights through exploratory data analysis, data cleaning, SQL queries, and an interactive Power BI dashboard.

The analysis explores customer demographics, spending patterns, product preferences, discounts, subscriptions, and shipping choices.
Dataset
The dataset contains 3,900 customer records and 18 columns.
Data Area	Attributes
Customer demographics	Customer ID, age, gender, and location
Purchase details	Item purchased, category, purchase amount, size, color, and season
Shopping behavior	Previous purchases, purchase frequency, and subscription status
Discounts	Discount applied and promo code used
Other details	Shipping type, payment method, and review rating


The dataset covers 25 products across four categories: Clothing, Accessories, Footwear, and Outerwear.

Project Workflow

1. Data Exploration
- Loaded the CSV dataset using pandas.
- Inspected data types, dimensions, and summary statistics.
- Examined categorical and numerical attributes.
- Identified 37 missing values in the review rating column.
2. Data Cleaning and Feature Engineering
- Filled missing review ratings using the mean rating of the corresponding product category.
- Standardized column names to snake_case.
- Renamed the purchase amount column to purchase_amount_usd.
- Created an age_group column using age quartiles.
- Added a purchase_frequency_days column to represent purchase frequency as approximate day intervals.
- Confirmed that promo code usage matched discount usage across all records and removed the redundant promo_code_used column.
3. Database Loading
- Connected Python to PostgreSQL using SQLAlchemy and psycopg2.
- Loaded the prepared dataset into the customer table in the customer_behavior database.
4. SQL Analysis
Answered business questions using aggregations, subqueries, conditional logic, common table expressions, and window functions.
5. Dashboard Development
Built a Power BI dashboard to explore customer segments, category performance, subscriptions, and purchasing patterns.

Business Questions
1. What is the total purchase revenue generated by male and female customers?
2. Which customers used a discount but spent more than the overall average purchase amount?
3. Which five products have the highest average review ratings?
4. How do average purchase amounts compare between Standard and Express shipping?
5. Do subscribers spend more than non-subscribers?
6. Which five products have the highest percentage of discounted purchases?
7. How many customers fall into New, Returning, and Loyalty segments?
8. What are the three most purchased products within each category?
9. How many repeat buyers have subscriptions?
10. What is the revenue contribution of each age group?

Power BI Dashboard
The dashboard includes:
- KPI cards: Customer count, average purchase amount, and average review rating.
- Subscription distribution: Share of subscribers and non-subscribers.
- Category analysis: Revenue and purchase counts by product category.
- Age group analysis: Revenue and purchase counts across age groups.
- Interactive slicers: Gender, subscription status, product category, and shipping type.

Key Findings
- Clothing generated the most revenue: $104,264, approximately 44.7% of total recorded purchase revenue.
- Subscribers had a slightly lower average purchase amount: $59.49 compared with $59.87 for non-subscribers.
- Young Adults contributed the most revenue among age groups: Customers aged 18–31 generated $62,143 and represented the largest group, with 1,028 records.
- Express shipping purchases had a higher average value: $60.48 compared with $58.46 for Standard shipping.
- Hats had the highest share of discounted purchases: Discounts were applied to 50% of recorded hat purchases.
These findings describe patterns in the dataset and do not establish causal relationships or measured business improvements.
Potential Business Applications
- Use category performance to guide further merchandising and inventory analysis.
- Investigate subscription benefits and engagement, given the similar average spending of subscribers and non-subscribers.
- Evaluate promotion performance using margin and campaign data before expanding discounts.
- Compare customer segments using both customer counts and average spending to account for differences in group size.
